Font of the mirror

Page 3

The night was showing its claws.

Bramwell woke around 3 a.m.

A nightmare.

You see, dear reader, I've been having this one particular dream for quite some time now—ever since something happened.

But that part of the story has not earned its voice yet.

This dream of mine was always accompanied by the warmth of moonlight above clouded skies. The swollen bodies of white, which always seemed to look down upon me, were watching me in my moment of glory.

Instead of the white night the moon usually greets us with, this one was rather colourful—in a dark way: lilac skies mixed with the promise of yellow thunder, backed by never-ending darkness.

There was an impossibly tall building.

Its architecture was simple, as if not to take my mind away from something far more important—four pillars guarding the glass floors. A simple building whose bottom seemed to have no end, one that we were challenging with our wings.

At the very top stood four pillars, curving toward one another and forming a platform at their meeting point.

We flew there.

For a moment, even that height was not enough for us.

We wanted more.

To leave the platform behind. To rise above the clouds, above the pillars, above everything that had ever looked down upon us.

Higher.

Still higher.

Until there was nothing left above us but the darkness itself.

We wanted to leap.

And soar…

Seven times I had this dream, and every single time I jumped, I felt the heaviness of something pull me down.

But the last time was different.

I soared.

I actually soared.

And when I woke, something I had thought lost to silence was no longer silent.

You see, dear reader, one's nightmare is sometimes nothing more than a long-forgotten dream. The dream did not change. The man did.

Bramwell had been thinking about going on a vacation for quite some time now. For some reason, no one at work would question his choice either.

His eyes had grown heavy from the constant sight of chaotic streets ending in confining rooms that offered no space to breathe. There is something about the beach that makes a man feel free. Perhaps it is the distant clouds meeting the never-ending sea, where far-gone ideas suddenly seem close enough to touch. Perhaps it is simply the illusion that, standing before something so endless, one might become endless too.

Bramwell longed for that kind of feeling—the kind that promises something fresh, something new. Perhaps this was only a glimpse of the change he had been waiting for.

The choice of Brighton was not random either. There are places one visits, and places one returns to without ever having truly left them.

I am not the kind of man to plan things out. I prefer to face things on impulse. So I went to Brighton as soon as I could.

The weather seemed to be on my side once again. The sun overwhelmed the clouds before I could even taste the morning. The beautiful nightmare was enough to make me overlook the sleepless night.

I notified my employer of my vacation, and it was approved almost immediately.

It was warm outside. I wore the suit that tends to kill any interest in the approving eyes of passersby. It is quite strange—I haven't worn shorts outside in at least seven years, and today was not going to be any different.

I packed everything I needed: two pairs of swimming shorts and the bare necessities to survive seven days in an unfamiliar place.

The sun annoyed the very journey I had been looking forward to. It seemed to be more excited than I was.
